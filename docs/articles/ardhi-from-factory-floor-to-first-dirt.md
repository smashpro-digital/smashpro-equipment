<p><strong>At a glance:</strong> SP-ARDHI-26 — "ARDHI" — is SmashPro's flagship compact tracked loader, factory model YF380. Built and tested overseas, shipped across the ocean, delivered October 26, 2026, and put to his first real dirt work on October 31. He is in field evaluation; commissioning approval is still ahead of him.</p>

<h2>Built on the line</h2>
<figure>
  <img src="https://smashpro.app/equipment/images/sp-ardhi-26-assembly-in-progress-web.jpg" alt="Partially assembled green YF380 mini skid steer on a factory assembly floor" width="691" height="600" loading="lazy" />
  <figcaption>Mid-assembly: loader frame and chassis joined before panels and hydraulics went on. (Manufacturer photo; capture date not recorded.)</figcaption>
</figure>
<p>ARDHI started as a frame on the YF380 assembly line. The manufacturer documented the build as it progressed — powertrain going in, the three-pump hydraulic system plumbed, panels and the SmashPro green applied.</p>
<figure>
  <img src="https://smashpro.app/equipment/images/sp-ardhi-26-hydraulic-system-installation-web.jpg" alt="Hydraulic pumps, hoses and valves being fitted during factory assembly" width="1280" height="1707" loading="lazy" />
  <figcaption>Hydraulic installation on the three-pump platform — the system that would move his first bucket of dirt two months later.</figcaption>
</figure>
<p>Before export approval, the factory ran function tests with the loader arms up and recorded a walkaround of the finished machine. The engine label we later photographed at receiving identifies a Runtong 739&nbsp;cc twin-cylinder gasoline engine; the exact model designation is still being confirmed with the manufacturer.</p>

<h2>Across the ocean</h2>
<figure>
  <img src="https://smashpro.app/equipment/images/sp-ardhi-26-shipping-2026-08-21-01-web.jpg" alt="Green mini skid steer secured on a wooden shipping pallet" width="1366" height="1024" loading="lazy" />
  <figcaption>August 21: strapped to the export pallet, bucket and auxiliary couplers visible, waiting for the crate.</figcaption>
</figure>
<p>On September 2 he left the factory. The ocean chapter ended with the documented arrival at the Port of Savannah in early October, followed by customs, the warehouse and a final-mile truck.</p>

<h2>Delivery day</h2>
<figure>
  <img src="https://smashpro.app/equipment/images/sp-ardhi-26-delivery-2026-10-26-carrier-and-crate-web.jpg" alt="Freight truck and wooden equipment crate on the ground at delivery" width="1920" height="1440" loading="lazy" />

</figure>
<figure>
  <img src="https://smashpro.app/equipment/images/sp-ardhi-26-unboxing-2026-10-26-crate-opened-web.jpg" alt="Opened wooden crate revealing the green mini skid steer" width="1440" height="1920" loading="lazy" />
  <figcaption>First look inside. One surprise waited under the pallet: a puncture in the left rubber track that the shipping base had hidden. The supplier describes sprocket-engagement openings as normal track design; we are monitoring it, and no warranty claim is confirmed.</figcaption>

<p>Uncrating was not without drama — the first start attempt failed before an oil-level correction and battery charging brought him to life (as reported by the owner; the root cause was never confirmed). The pallet forks were unpacked, assembled and mounted on the quick-change plate the next day.</p>

<h2>First dirt</h2>
<figure>
  <img src="https://smashpro.app/equipment/images/sp-ardhi-26-field-test-2026-10-31-01-web.jpg" alt="Mini skid steer parked near mulch bags with a playground in the background" width="1920" height="1440" loading="lazy" />
  <figcaption>October 31 — Dirt Day. Mulch staged, playground area waiting, dust already building.</figcaption>
</figure>
<p>Five days after delivery, ARDHI did his first real work: bucket passes, grading, dirt and material movement, and pallet-fork practice. Sessions that week total roughly five reported hours, including one stretch of more than three hours — operator-reported time, not a verified hour-meter reading.</p>
