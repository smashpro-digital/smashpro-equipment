<ul>
  <li><strong>First start failed, then recovered.</strong> The initial attempt on delivery day was unsuccessful. After an oil-level correction (an overfill, by owner report) and battery charging, he started and has started reliably since. The original failure's root cause was never confirmed, so the record keeps both facts.</li>
  <li><strong>Left-track puncture.</strong> Found only after the crate came off — the pallet had hidden it. The supplier describes sprocket-engagement openings as normal track design; we are monitoring for actual tearing and no warranty claim is confirmed. Stated warranty coverage is frame, engine and hydraulic pump, with consumables excluded.</li>
  <li><strong>Pallet forks assembled.</strong> Unpacked, assembled and mounted on the quick-change plate, with a receiving function test on video.</li>
</ul>

<h2>The two open issues</h2>
<figure>
  <img src="https://smashpro.app/equipment/images/sp-ardhi-26-field-qa-control-panel-2026-10-31-web.jpg" alt="Control panel with throttle lever and toothed quadrant" width="1440" height="1920" loading="lazy" />
  <figcaption>The throttle quadrant. The lever stops at roughly half travel; diagnosis comes before adjustment.</figcaption>
</figure>
<ol>
  <li><strong>Throttle travel restriction — open.</strong> After Dirt Day the throttle lever physically stops at about half its travel. The cause is unverified; the rule on this machine is diagnose first, never force the lever or touch governor stops. A universal throttle cable kit was ordered November 2 as a candidate part — fitment unverified, not installed.</li>
  <li><strong>Quick-change pin retaining screw — owner-reported repair.</strong> One locking pin lost its screw after field use, disabling its spring return. M6 replacement dog-point set screws were ordered November 2, and the owner subsequently reported the repair complete with an additional screw added to <em>both</em> pins. That is an owner report: independent retention and function verification is still required before loaded attachment work, and the replacement screw specification is not manufacturer-approved.</li>
</ol>

<h2>What verification still needs</h2>
<ul>
  <li>Throttle diagnosis and an approved repair path.</li>
  <li>Retention/function verification of both quick-change pins.</li>
  <li>Oil and hydraulic-fluid baselines against confirmed manufacturer specifications.</li>
  <li>Battery and charging-system inspection — reliable starts alone do not verify battery health.</li>
  <li>Filter identification and grease-point mapping once manual applicability is confirmed.</li>
  <li>A verified hour-meter baseline; current time is operator-reported (~5 hours).</li>
</ul>
