# SP-ARDHI-26 Build & Purchase Experience

> **Purpose:** Preserve the practical history of how SP-ARDHI-26 was selected, negotiated, customized, paid for, documented, and followed through production. This is a project-history and lessons-learned record. The canonical procurement specifications remain in [SP-ARDHI-26-PROCUREMENT.md](SP-ARDHI-26-PROCUREMENT.md).

## 1. Project Intent

SP-ARDHI-26 was developed as a direct-from-factory compact tracked loader / mini skid steer acquisition intended to become a branded SmashPro fleet asset rather than a stock resale machine.

The project combined four goals:

1. Acquire a compact tracked work platform for landscaping, material handling, cleanup, grading, and attachment-driven work.
2. Configure the machine around real SmashPro field use rather than simply accepting a marketplace listing as-is.
3. Establish a repeatable process for buying customized equipment directly from an overseas manufacturer.
4. Turn the procurement and build process itself into documented fleet knowledge, media, and future purchasing leverage.

The OEM/factory model documented for the project is **YF380**. The SmashPro fleet identity is **SP-ARDHI-26**, with the working name **Ardhi**.

## 2. Supplier & Buying Channel

The purchase was handled through **Alibaba** with **Shandong Infront Machinery Co., Ltd.** as the seller. Communication during the build was handled through the supplier representative known in the project conversation as **Hailey**.

The transaction behaved more like managing a small custom manufacturing project than buying a finished retail machine. The buyer had to define the configuration, branding, included accessories, documentation expectations, payment sequence, production proof, and shipping follow-up.

### What this means for future purchases

The Alibaba product page should be treated as the commercial doorway, not as the complete specification. The actual build must be controlled through written confirmations, invoice/specification documents, production photos, and a maintained procurement record.

## 3. Baseline Machine Selection

The selected platform was the **YF380 compact tracked loader**.

Documented core specification from the procurement record:

| Model | YF380 |
| Engine | Runtong twin-cylinder gasoline |
| Displacement | 739 cc |
| Rated horsepower | 23 HP |
| Certification | EPA certified |
| Hydraulic system on signed procurement document | Double pump / double valve |
| Tracks | Rubber tracked undercarriage |

The machine was selected as a flexible base platform rather than for one narrow job. That mattered because the long-term value of the asset depends on the number of useful tasks and attachments it can support.

## 4. Build Configuration Strategy

The build evolved through active discussion with the factory rather than remaining a stock YF380 order.

### Hydraulic configuration

The signed procurement documentation lists a **double-pump / double-valve system**. During the build, a three-pump upgrade was discussed and later production imagery appeared to show a triple-pump configuration.

That observation is intentionally kept separate from the signed specification until the factory supplies documentation confirming it.

### Controls, lighting, and counterweight discussions

Pilot-control possibilities, beacon/rear work lighting, and counterweight options were discussed during configuration. Any feature not explicitly confirmed in final factory documentation remains a requested/discussed feature rather than a canonical as-built specification.

## 5. Included Equipment & Attachment Decisions

The base procurement record includes standard bucket, toolbox, common tools, spare filter cartridge, spare belt, and pallet forks included at no additional charge despite a stated accessory value of $320.

The pallet forks were deliberately prioritized because they immediately expand the machine from a dirt-moving loader into a material-handling platform. Additional attachment categories discussed included grapple, rake, brush/bush cutting, and other future landscape/property-maintenance implements.

## 6. Branding & Visual Build

Approved direction included green primary paint, black contrasting areas, SmashPro branding, **SP-ARDHI-26** fleet identity, website/phone branding, and branded pallet forks requested as part of the visual package.

The branding process required reviewing factory mockups and production images rather than assuming the first layout would be final.

## 7. Commercial Structure

The signed proforma invoice establishes a **$3,990 USD DDP baseline build price**, with export wooden-crate packaging and transportation to the Hopkins, South Carolina delivery address included.

Pallet forks carry a stated $320 accessory value but were included at $0 additional charge on the invoice.

## 8. Split-Payment Strategy

A key part of the actual buying experience was that the supplier created **two separate Alibaba orders specifically so the buyer could split the purchase into smaller payments**. This was intentional payment flexibility, not an accidental duplicate order.

### Alibaba order A — #308705042001020107

This order carried a **$1,990 USD contract amount** and was paid in two completed card transactions:

- July 16, 2026: **$1,000 initial payment**
- July 23, 2026: **$990 balance payment**

### Alibaba order B — #307512355501020107

A later receipt for the second order shows **$2,269 USD of order amount**, plus a separately stated processing fee. The receipt records card charges of **$1,029.90 on August 6** and **$1,306.95 on August 20**, both to the same card ending 7914.

### Why the split mattered

The split-order arrangement made the imported-equipment purchase more manageable from a cash-flow standpoint. Instead of requiring one large marketplace payment, the seller accommodated staged payments through separate Alibaba order records.

That flexibility is an important part of the positive supplier experience and should be considered in future equipment negotiations when cash flow is a constraint.

### Accounting caution

The two marketplace order amounts currently preserved total **$4,259**, while the signed proforma invoice establishes a **$3,990 baseline DDP build price**. The extra **$269** should not be assigned to an upgrade, accessory, or fee unless separate evidence confirms it. Alibaba processing fees are also kept separate from equipment-order amounts.

The correct historical interpretation is therefore:

- two orders were intentionally created for payment splitting;
- both belong to the same SP-ARDHI-26 acquisition;
- the baseline invoice price remains $3,990 DDP;
- the $269 marketplace-order difference still needs documentary reconciliation.

## 9. Production Follow-Up Experience

Production update photos and video became an important control point. They helped verify the machine was being built, review paint and branding, inspect visible attachment/configuration details, catch apparent differences from the signed specification, and create a media trail before shipment.

The production imagery also surfaced the possible triple-pump change, demonstrating why build-update media should be treated as procurement evidence, not merely marketing content.

## 10. Documentation Experience

The project now separates canonical documented specification, observed production changes, payment evidence, production media, shipping documents, warranty/protection evidence, and future service/maintenance records.

That structure prevents details from becoming trapped in Alibaba messages, screenshots, invoices, or memory.

## 11. Shipping & Delivery Experience

The purchase was negotiated as **DDP**. The original procurement estimate referenced approximately 30 days after payment. The Alibaba interface later displayed the order as shipped and showed a 50-day delivery indicator.

Final logistics history should be based on freight documents, carrier/tracking information, arrival date, and actual delivery condition.

## 12. Warranty & Service Protection

The vendor procurement terms document a **1-year or 1,000-operating-hour warranty, whichever occurs first**.

Separately, the Alibaba order interface shows **On-site service — Status: Covered**, with 1 year of on-site service, free replacement parts, and an after-delivery service-request path. The captured page also states platform compensation of 2% of product amount, up to US $500, for failed service under the displayed terms.

This creates a useful second service layer beyond informal supplier support.

## 13. What Worked Well

Strong points in the acquisition included direct factory communication, customized configuration, pallet forks included, staged/split-payment accommodation, factory progress media, pre-shipment branding, marketplace protection, and building a formal equipment passport/procurement trail from the beginning.

## 14. Friction & Risks Encountered

The main risks were specification drift, fragmented payment evidence across multiple Alibaba orders/receipts, generic marketplace listing descriptions, and documentation lag for final component IDs, QC, EPA, freight, and serial-number records.

The split-order arrangement itself was useful, but it reinforces the need for a dedicated payment ledger tying every marketplace order number to one canonical asset purchase.

## 15. Improvements for the Next China Build

For future custom equipment purchases:

1. Lock a pre-order build sheet before payment.
2. Keep one commercial summary for baseline price, add-ons, freight, and fees.
3. If split orders are used, document **before the first payment** that all order numbers belong to one acquisition and state the purpose of each order.
4. Save payment evidence immediately after every transaction.
5. Require written change-order confirmation for material factory changes.
6. Request dated production checkpoints at build, paint/branding, final assembly, QC, and packing.
7. Capture serial numbers, engine identification, manuals, EPA material, final weight, and freight documentation before/at shipment where available.
8. Photograph and inspect the machine on arrival before field work.
9. Reconcile the physical machine against the final procurement record.
10. Preserve marketplace warranty/protection pages before and after delivery.

## 16. Overall Purchase Assessment to Date

SP-ARDHI-26 demonstrates that a small business can use Alibaba to source a genuinely customized fleet asset while also negotiating a workable payment structure. The supplier's willingness to split the transaction across two Alibaba orders was operationally valuable because it made the purchase easier to manage without changing the fact that both orders belonged to one machine acquisition.

The strongest lesson remains documentation discipline. The machine, invoice, split Alibaba orders, receipts, production photos, supplier messages, shipping records, warranty terms, and marketplace protection each tell a different part of the same story. Keeping them linked creates a trustworthy asset history and a stronger template for the next imported fleet build.
