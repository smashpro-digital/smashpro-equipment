# SP-ARDHI-26 Build & Purchase Experience

> **Purpose:** Preserve the practical history of how SP-ARDHI-26 was selected, negotiated, customized, paid for, documented, and followed through production. This is a project-history and lessons-learned record. The canonical procurement specifications remain in [SP-ARDHI-26-PROCUREMENT.md](SP-ARDHI-26-PROCUREMENT.md).

## 1. Project Intent

SP-ARDHI-26 was developed as a direct-from-factory compact tracked loader / mini skid steer acquisition intended to become a branded SmashPro fleet asset rather than a stock resale machine.

The project combined four goals:

1. Acquire a compact tracked work platform for landscaping, material handling, cleanup, grading, and attachment-driven work.
2. Configure the machine around real SmashPro field use rather than simply accepting a marketplace listing as-is.
3. Establish a repeatable process for buying customized equipment directly from an overseas manufacturer.
4. Turn the procurement and build process itself into documented fleet knowledge, media, and future purchasing leverage.

The OEM/factory model documented for the project is **YF380**. The SmashPro fleet identity is **SP-ARDHI-26**, with the working name **Ardhi**.

## 2. Supplier & Buying Channel

The purchase was handled through **Alibaba** with **Shandong Infront Machinery Co., Ltd.** as the seller. Communication during the build was handled through the supplier representative known in the project conversation as **Hailey**.

The transaction behaved more like managing a small custom manufacturing project than buying a finished retail machine. The buyer had to define the configuration, branding, included accessories, documentation expectations, payment sequence, production proof, and shipping follow-up.

### What this means for future purchases

The Alibaba product page should be treated as the commercial doorway, not as the complete specification. The actual build must be controlled through written confirmations, invoice/specification documents, production photos, and a maintained procurement record.

## 4. Build Configuration Strategy

The build evolved through active discussion with the factory rather than remaining a stock YF380 order.

### Hydraulic configuration

The signed procurement documentation lists a **double-pump / double-valve system**. During the build, a three-pump upgrade was discussed and later production imagery appeared to show a triple-pump configuration.

That observation is intentionally kept separate from the signed specification until the factory supplies documentation confirming it. This became one of the most important documentation lessons from the project: production photographs can show a meaningful improvement, but they should not silently overwrite the contractual build record.

### Controls and operating configuration

Pilot-control and other operator-control possibilities were discussed during configuration. Any feature that is not explicitly confirmed in the final factory documents should remain a requested or discussed feature rather than a confirmed machine specification.

### Lighting and field visibility

Beacon lighting and rear work-light capability were part of the build discussion. The goal was to make the machine more usable and visible around real field operations, loading, unloading, and lower-light work rather than focusing only on cosmetic customization.

### Counterweight and capacity discussions

Counterweight options were also explored as part of understanding how much practical material-handling capability could be extracted from the compact platform. As with hydraulic changes, final as-built capacity should ultimately be governed by factory documentation and physical verification after delivery.

## 5. Included Equipment & Attachment Decisions

The base procurement record includes:

- standard bucket;
- toolbox;
- common tools;
- spare filter cartridge;
- spare belt;
- pallet forks included at no additional charge despite a stated accessory value of $320.

The pallet forks were deliberately prioritized because they immediately expand the machine from a dirt-moving loader into a material-handling platform.

Additional attachment categories discussed during the purchasing process included grapple, rake, brush/bush cutting, and other future landscape or property-maintenance implements. The strategy was not to buy every attachment at once. Instead, the machine and its first attachments were selected to create a useful operating baseline, with later attachments added after real-world use identifies the best return.

## 6. Branding & Visual Build

The factory customization was designed to make SP-ARDHI-26 recognizable as a SmashPro fleet asset from day one.

Approved direction included:

- green primary machine color;
- black contrasting areas;
- SmashPro logo/branding;
- **SP-ARDHI-26** fleet ID on the machine;
- website branding on a black panel;
- phone-number branding across a green surface;
- branded pallet forks requested as part of the overall visual package.

The branding process required reviewing factory mockups and production images rather than assuming the first layout would be final. Placement, scale, readability, and how the graphics worked across different machine panels all mattered.

## 8. Payment Experience

The payment process was staged rather than treated as one single card charge.

A receipt issued August 31, 2026 for the same Alibaba order number documents:

| Receipt entry | Amount |
| --- | ---: |
| Receipt order amount | $2,269.00 |
| Payment processing fee | $67.85 |
| **Amount represented on that receipt** | **$2,336.85** |
| August 6, 2026 card payment | $1,029.90 |
| August 20, 2026 card payment | $1,306.95 |

Because the Alibaba order page independently shows the complete order total as **$3,990**, the $2,269 receipt amount should not be interpreted as the full equipment purchase price. It is preserved as payment evidence for the portion represented by that receipt. A complete payment reconciliation should retain every Alibaba receipt/payment record associated with the order.

### Purchase-process lesson

Marketplace receipts, order totals, processing fees, and negotiated factory pricing can appear in different documents and may not present the transaction in the same way. Future purchases should maintain a simple payment ledger from the first payment forward instead of reconstructing the transaction at the end.

## 9. Production Follow-Up Experience

The factory relationship continued after payment rather than going quiet until shipment. Production update photos and video became an important control point.

Those updates served several purposes:

- verify that an actual machine was being built;
- review SmashPro paint and branding;
- inspect visible equipment and attachment details;
- catch differences between quoted and apparent as-built configuration;
- create a media trail for the equipment project;
- maintain momentum with the supplier before shipment.

The production imagery also surfaced the possible triple-pump change, demonstrating why build-update media should be treated as procurement evidence, not merely marketing content.

## 10. Documentation Experience

A major goal became creating an equipment passport and procurement record instead of allowing details to remain scattered across Alibaba messages, images, invoices, and chat history.

The project now separates information into categories:

- **canonical documented specification** from signed/vendor documents;
- **observed production changes** that still need confirmation;
- **payment evidence** from Alibaba;
- **production media**;
- **shipping documents** once received;
- **future service and maintenance records** after arrival.

This structure prevents a common problem with imported equipment: several months later, nobody remembers which detail came from a listing, a salesperson, an invoice, or the machine itself.

## 11. Shipping & Delivery Experience

The purchase was negotiated as **DDP**, reducing the amount of import logistics the buyer expected to manage independently.

The original procurement estimate referenced approximately 30 days after payment. The Alibaba order interface later displayed the order as **shipped** and showed a **delivered in 50 days** indicator.

Shipping remains a phase where the project record should preserve the actual bill of lading, container/tracking information, carrier information, arrival date, and final delivery condition once those documents are available. Marketplace status text should not substitute for the underlying freight documentation.

## 12. What Worked Well

Several parts of the acquisition created meaningful value:

- negotiating a customized machine instead of accepting the stock listing configuration;
- getting pallet forks included;
- establishing direct communication with a factory representative;
- receiving factory progress photos/video;
- customizing the machine before shipment rather than retrofitting all branding locally;
- using a formal fleet ID from the beginning;
- maintaining a procurement passport rather than relying on marketplace history;
- preserving the proforma invoice and Alibaba payment evidence separately;
- treating discrepancies as items to reconcile instead of forcing documents to match.

## 13. Friction & Risks Encountered

The project also exposed several areas that need tighter controls on future imports.

### Specification drift

A discussed or photographed upgrade can differ from the signed invoice. Every material configuration change should ideally result in an updated invoice, signed build sheet, or manufacturer confirmation.

### Fragmented payment evidence

The Alibaba order page shows a $3,990 total while the August 31 receipt represents only $2,269 of order amount plus its processing fee. Without maintaining all payment documents together, that can look like a contradiction even though both records reference the same order.

### Marketplace listing descriptions

The Alibaba receipt/product description is generic and includes broad excavator-related listing language that does not accurately function as a technical description of the custom SP-ARDHI-26 build. For equipment records, the negotiated invoice and verified machine specifications must take precedence over marketplace SEO/listing text.

### Documentation lag

Some useful information, such as final engine model, engine serial number, hydraulic pump identifiers, serial number, QC report, EPA documentation, and freight documents, may arrive later than the purchase agreement. The procurement record therefore needs to remain a living document through delivery and commissioning.

## 14. Improvements for the Next China Build

For future custom equipment purchases, use a tighter gate-based process:

1. **Pre-order build sheet:** lock model, engine, hydraulics, controls, dimensions, weight, attachments, paint, decals, electrical options, warranty, documentation, and shipping terms before payment.
2. **Single commercial summary:** record negotiated equipment price, accessory values, shipping, fees, and expected payment schedule in one ledger.
3. **Payment evidence after every transaction:** save the Alibaba payment confirmation/receipt immediately and update the ledger.
4. **Written change orders:** require written confirmation for any factory change after the order is placed.
5. **Production checkpoints:** request dated photos/video at chassis/build, paint/branding, final assembly, QC, and packing stages.
6. **Pre-shipment document gate:** do not treat the build as documentation-complete until serial numbers, engine identification, manuals, EPA material, packing details, final weight, and shipping paperwork are captured where available.
7. **Arrival inspection:** photograph the crate before opening and verify model, serial numbers, fluids, damage, loose hardware, attachments, controls, hydraulic functions, safety equipment, and branded graphics before field work.
8. **Post-arrival reconciliation:** compare the physical machine against the final procurement record and record every variance.
9. **Protection capture:** save screenshots of marketplace protection, warranty, and service-benefit pages before delivery so claim terms are not dependent on a later UI state.

## 15. Overall Purchase Assessment to Date

SP-ARDHI-26 demonstrates that a small business can use Alibaba to source a genuinely customized fleet asset, but the value comes from actively managing the transaction rather than simply placing an online order.

The strongest part of the experience has been the ability to communicate directly with the manufacturer, influence the build, receive production updates, include useful accessories, and create a machine that arrives with a distinct SmashPro identity.

The main operational lesson is documentation discipline. The machine, invoice, marketplace order, receipts, production photos, supplier messages, shipping records, warranty terms, and marketplace protection records each tell a different part of the story. Keeping those layers separate but linked creates a trustworthy asset history and a much stronger template for the next imported fleet build.

## 17. Warranty & Service-Protection Experience

The order has two distinct protection layers in the current record and they should not be blended together.

The manufacturer/vendor procurement terms document a **1-year or 1,000-operating-hour warranty, whichever occurs first**, with misuse and consumable damage excluded.

Separately, the Alibaba order-detail interface shows **On-site service — Status: Covered**. The captured protection page states that the order has **1 year of on-site service and free replacement parts**, and that a service request can be started after the product has been delivered.

The Alibaba page also states that if service fails, platform compensation may be available at **2% of the product amount, up to US $500**. The workflow shown is to apply through **My orders > Order details**; the seller then provides a solution or the buyer may become eligible for compensation under the displayed protection terms.

### Why this matters operationally

For an imported machine, the existence of a platform-backed service layer is meaningful because the practical risk is not only whether a warranty exists on paper, but whether there is a usable path to parts and resolution after the machine is thousands of miles from the factory.

The service-protection screenshot gives the Ardhi purchase a clearer escalation path than relying solely on informal supplier messages. If a real service event occurs, the project should document the issue from the first symptom forward, open the Alibaba service request while the coverage window is active, preserve every seller response, and keep proof of any replacement parts or compensation.

### Arrival follow-up

Once the machine is physically delivered, verify that the Alibaba order still shows the protection as active and capture the post-delivery claim interface. That will turn the current pre-delivery protection screenshot into a complete service-readiness record.

This document should be updated again after freight documentation, arrival, commissioning, and the first real field-use period so the record captures not only the buying experience but whether the delivered machine ultimately matched the promise.
